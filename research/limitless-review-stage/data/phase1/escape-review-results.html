Fresh defects confirm three reviewers beat production, and a cheap one keeps up

Overnight, every candidate reviewed the 51 dev escape cases twice. Those cases hold 80 real defects that production review let through, labelled on 30 October and never used to tune anything. Recall is judged by two models, Claude Sonnet 5.5 and GPT-6.1 Terra, and a defect counts as caught only when both agree it was.

What it means

  • Production catches about one escape defect in six. These are the defects it already missed once, so its baseline here sits below its 31.6% on the old dev set.
  • Three reviewers clear the +10-point target on unseen defects: D, Sol @high and E3.
  • Terra 6.1 with the adversarial prompt matches E3 and comes close to Sol @high, at about a sixth of production's cost and a third of its time.
  • Luna doesn't work as a reviewer (2.5% and 4.4%).
  • Haiku 5.5 fails the 20-minute rule: 17 of 32 reviews ran over 20 minutes, with 5 timeouts or stalls.
  • Every Sol and Terra reviewer stays far under 20 minutes; the slowest p90 is 5.3 minutes.

What this run can't tell us

  • False blocks. Every escape case has a defect, so blocking clean code isn't measured here. D doubled true clean blocks on the held-out clean set (4 of 20 against 2 of 20), and nobody has tested Terra on clean cases yet.
  • Held-out confirmation. None of this touches the 57 held-out escape cases. Adopting anything should rest on them.

Proposed decision run

One confirmation run on the held-out escape cases (103 defects) plus the held-out clean set, with production, Sol @high, E3, Terra-adversarial, D, and a cheap union of two Terra-adversarial reviews (D's recipe at about $0.06 a review).

The decision rule is the one already agreed: at least 10 points of recall over production, and a rise of more than 50% in true clean blocks needs a considerable gain to pay for it.
