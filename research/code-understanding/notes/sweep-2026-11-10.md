## Findings

### A. White et al., folded into #2, #1, and the shipped hold-back

- **Paper.** "Feeling Easier, Working Harder: Cognitive Load in AI-Assisted Code Comprehension," White, Rose, Buckman, McDonnell, Ortega, and Trinkenreich, Colorado State University. arXiv 2611.12019v1, published 2026-11-09T07:56:58Z. The arXiv comment says "Accepted to ICSE 2027."
- **Design, from the HTML.** 3×2 within-subjects. The abstract says 20 novice CS students. The methods say 22 people completed the session and 2 were excluded (one for a technical failure, one who did not use the AI), leaving N=20 software engineering students from two Java courses. Table I includes 15% with 5 or more years of Java. Snippets were written for the study: about 30, 45, and 70 lines. The AI was the public web interface of chat-based Gemini, on an adjacent second monitor. The code and the quiz stayed on the laptop. Each task had four multiple-choice questions, one each for maintenance, summarization, mental execution, and structure (the results section says "structural mapping"), from the Wyrich et al. framework. The quiz ran with the AI closed.
- **Workload.** Model 1: AI assistance raised tDRT reaction times 11.2% (chi-square 17.37, df 1, p<.001). During reading the gap was 12.7% (p<.001). In the quiz phase, after the AI was gone, the results section says the gap was 6.9% (p=0.007). NASA-TLX p=0.180. SEQ p=0.161. Both subjective scales were unchanged.
- **Quiz.** Table V: mean accuracy 3.22 (SD 0.99) with AI and 3.23 (SD 1.08) without, out of 4. A perfect score on all four questions was 50.0% of AI tasks and 58.3% of no-AI tasks. The planned model was chi-square(1)<0.01, p=.982. An ordinal sensitivity analysis was p=.692. The paper calls the cluster at the top a ceiling effect, and says this is an absence of evidence for a benefit at these difficulties.
- **Themes.** Resistance, Scaffolding, and Displacement. The RQ3 summary says roughly a quarter let the explanations displace direct code reading. One quoted line: "I then almost exclusively read the LLM output instead of the code." The paper does not give an exact headcount for that quarter.
- **Evidence.** A within-subjects measurement on a small student sample. Order of code size was randomized, and AI was counterbalanced inside each person. People were not assigned to separate groups. It is not tagged `measured-RCT`.
- **Decision.** On #2: self-reported ease did not move while the reaction-time cost did, and this quiz did not separate the conditions. Self-reported ease and time-to-approve stay off the comprehension signal. The quiz and the extend-by-hand task stay the guard, and the quiz has to be hard enough to leave the ceiling. On #1: the four-question set, taken after the reader's own pass with the AI closed, is a usable template, with harder mental-execution questions than this ceiling. On the shipped hold-back row: Displacement is the failure that row exists to prevent. Considered for #9. The study does not vary explanation depth, so it is not cited there. No new row.

### B. Jane Street `socratic-tutor`, folded into #3, #1, and #9

- **Skill.** [SKILL.md](https://github.com/janestreet/agent_skills/blob/master/socratic-tutor/SKILL.md) on `master`. The repo is MIT. GitHub API `stargazers_count` 3136 at 2026-11-10T13:20:56Z and 3140 at 2026-11-10T13:24:25Z, `forks_count` 185, created 2026-11-03T16:50:21Z. The backlog uses 3140. The description says the skill is for an explicit teach, quiz, or drill session, and not for a one-off "explain X" or "what is X". If the intent is ambiguous it asks once before the diagnostic.
- **Mechanics, from that file.** A topic becomes 4–10 subskills. A diagnostic of 3–5 questions, easy to hard, runs before any teaching and places a level per subskill. Levels are 0 unaware, 1 heard of it, 2 can follow along, 3 can do with help, 4 can do independently, 5 can teach it. The map is rendered after every level change. One question per message, and the message ends at the question. A wrong answer gets a narrower question that exposes the contradiction. A direct explanation waits until 2–3 failed tries, then the same idea is re-tested from another angle. A level rises only after 2 or more independent checks, at least one of them unprompted recall. Spaced re-tests can drop a level. The course ends when every subskill is at the target (default 4/5) and a mixed final assessment is passed. One exercise example is "predict what this code prints." The triggers are teach, tutor, quiz, drill, and master, for any topic.
- **What `teach` already contributes, and what this adds.** The backlog already cites `teach`'s interview, its learning records, its spaced return of earlier quiz items, its equal-length quiz options, and the reported gap that `teach` never grills for a starting level. This skill adds the intent guard, the 3–5 question placement, the rendered 0–5 map as the state the learner sees, earned levels with an unprompted-recall rule, and a re-test that can lower a level.
